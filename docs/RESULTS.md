# Results — 600-step controlled comparison

Measured from the machine-saved evidence in `results/language_modeling/` (`*_600_log.json`).
Values at the final evaluation, step 500 of the 600-step run. Single run per
family, same seed unless stated.

| Family | Params | Train loss @500 | **Val loss @500** | ~rate (M tok/s) | ~600-step wall time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `attn` (Transformer) | 0.92M | 2.172 | **2.201** | ~0.35 | ~25 min |
| `linattn` (fixed decay) | 0.92M | 1.836 | **2.003** | ~0.40 | ~22 min |
| `decayattn` (learned forget) | 0.92M | 1.592 | **1.792** | ~0.25 | ~34 min |

Config: `BATCH 64, SEQ 192, EMBED 160, N_LAYER 3, N_HEAD 8, HEAD_DIM 20,
MLP 576, DROP 0.1, LR 3e-4 (AdamW), CLIP 1.0, EVAL_EVERY 500`.

## Reading the numbers

- Validation loss is cross-entropy on a held-out 10% of the corpus that none of
  the three models ever trained on.
- Lower is better. `decayattn` beats `linattn` by 0.21 nats and the Transformer
  by 0.41 nats at the same parameter count and same total training budget.
- This is a **clean within-budget comparison**, which is exactly what a
  controlled Pareto study needs; it is **not** evidence that learned decay is a
  new discovery (see `NOVELTY.md`).

## Corroboration checks performed

1. **Math check:** the closed-form (decay-matrix) implementation was verified to
   equal the naive recurrence scan; max absolute difference ~9.3e-9. The fast
   code is not silently a different model.
2. **Random init control:** untrained model output is indistinguishable noise;
   trained output is coherent Shakespeare-shaped text, confirming training
   signal, not initialization luck.
3. **Seed robustness (seeds 1-3):** full repeatability run with three
   independent seeds (fresh init, batching, and probe instances) at the
   identical frozen protocol. Decision rules were frozen in
   `docs/SEED_ROBUSTNESS.md` before any run. **Verdict: PASS on every seed.**

Step-500 validation loss across all four seeds (0 original + 1-3 new):

| Family | seed 0 | seed 1 | seed 2 | seed 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `decayattn` (learned forget) | **1.7925** | **1.8051** | **1.8081** | **1.7978** |
| `linattn` (fixed decay) | 2.0035 | 2.0022 | 1.9931 | 2.0147 |
| `attn` (Transformer) | 2.2007 | 2.2131 | 2.1698 | 2.1992 |

The strict ranking `decayattn < linattn < attn` holds in all four seeds, with
every gap positive (0.18-0.22 nats). Raw logs: `results/language_modeling/*_600_seed*_log.json`.

## Limitations (read before believing too much)

- Small scale: 0.92M params, 600 steps, char-level, 1M-token corpus. Do not
  extrapolate to large-scale conclusions.
- Single configuration and single dataset; no downstream task, no exact-match /
  retrieval eval.
- Results are reported across four seeds (0 original + 1-3); the ordering holds
  in every seed and matches the published literature (FoX), which increases
  trust but is not a guarantee.
- `decayattn` is the slowest of the three to train in this closed form.

## Raw evidence

Each `results/language_modeling/*_600_log.json` records per-step training loss, elapsed seconds,
and validation loss at step 500. Checkpoints `.pt` are intentionally excluded
(small, and trivially regenerated by the documented commands).

## Recall probe (v3, final)

Same families, same 0.92M budget, 1500 steps, but the question is **exact
one-shot associative recall** (find the value paired with a key earlier in the
context) instead of next-token prediction. Protocol and the full version
history (including two flaws we caught and fixed) are in `PROTOCOL.md`; the
full numbers are in `docs/PROBE_RESULTS.md`.

Headline: **the Transformer learns recall (~24% at R4, ~10x chance) while
both constant-memory families stay at the chance floor (~2%)**, and the same
ordering holds under heavier loads and across four independent seeds (see
`SEED_ROBUSTNESS.md`). The families that won the perplexity race
lose the recall race outright. See the "What this says" section in
`PROBE_RESULTS.md` for the honest boundary of this finding.
